# Hormuz-Shadow-Global-Shipping-Disruption-Analysis

## 1. Project Purpose and Motivation

The Strait of Hormuz is an important route for global shipping, especially for transporting oil and other energy resources. If access to this route is restricted due to geopolitical disruptions, ships may need to take longer alternative routes, which could increase travel time and fuel costs.

The purpose of this project is to understand how restricted access to the Strait of Hormuz could affect shipping distance, estimated travel time, and fuel costs using a simulated rerouting scenario.

To do this, I combined three Kaggle datasets covering ship movements, port activity, and fuel prices. I selected and merged the relevant columns to create a structured dataset for analysis. Using Python, I compared normal shipping conditions with a simulated disruption scenario to estimate how longer routes could affect travel time and fuel costs.



###  Objectives

- Compare shipping distance under baseline and simulated disruption scenarios.
- Estimate changes in travel time resulting from longer alternative routes.
- Quantify the estimated increase in fuel costs associated with rerouting.
- Investigate the relationship between shipping distance and estimated fuel costs.
- Apply statistical methods to evaluate differences between the scenarios.
- Visualize the conceptual route deviation and communicate its potential operational implications.

### Overall Interpretation

The main analytical contribution of this project is to demonstrate how a hypothetical maritime disruption can be translated into measurable operational changes using data integration, simulation, and quantitative analysis.

The model illustrates a logical chain of potential effects:

**Restricted route access → alternative routing → increased distance → increased estimated travel time and fuel costs.**

The distance and cost changes generated by the model are conditional on its assumptions. Real-world impacts would depend on the actual alternative route, vessel characteristics, fuel prices, operating conditions, and the duration of the disruption.

## 5. Business and Supply-Chain Implications

The analysis highlights several areas relevant to maritime logistics planning.

- **Operational cost planning:** Shipping operators can use scenario analysis to explore how longer routes might affect voyage budgets.
- **Route contingency planning:** Comparing alternative routes can help identify the operational trade-offs associated with disruption.
- **Delivery scheduling:** Potential increases in travel time may need to be considered when planning deliveries and vessel availability.
- **Supply-chain resilience:** Scenario modeling can help organizations prepare for risks affecting critical maritime chokepoints.
- **Decision support:** Quantitative comparisons provide a structured way to discuss possible disruption impacts, while making the underlying assumptions explicit.

These implications are exploratory. The project does not establish actual industry-wide cost increases or prove that a particular rerouting strategy is optimal.

## 6. Limitations and Future Improvements

The project uses simplified simulation assumptions and a deterministic cost model. It does not fully represent the complexity of real maritime operations.

Future work could improve the analysis by:

- Calculating alternative routes using realistic maritime navigation constraints.
- Incorporating vessel-specific fuel consumption and speed.
- Matching fuel prices to the relevant location, date, and fuel type.
- Accounting for weather, congestion, and port waiting times.
- Testing several distance multipliers through sensitivity analysis.
- Using observed voyage data to validate modeled travel distances and times.
- Introducing uncertainty ranges to estimate how results change under different assumptions.

These improvements would help distinguish the direct effects of the simulation assumptions from the more complex effects that could occur in a real-world disruption.
